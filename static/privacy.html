Privacy, in plain words

This familiar puts a microphone on your table. Everything below is what that actually means — written to be read, not to be agreed to.

What is recorded, and when

Questions. When somebody says the familiar's name, the words after it are sent to this server, answered, and both the question and the answer are kept in that table's session log.

Table talk. Separately, there is a record table talk switch. While it is on, everything the device hears is transcribed and sent to this server in chunks, whether or not anyone was talking to the familiar. This is how the timeline, the cast list, the places and the in-game calendar get written. The switch is off by default, lives on the device rather than on the account, and has to be turned on again for each session.

Where the audio goes

This server never receives audio. Speech is turned into text by the browser, and only text is sent. But the familiar uses the browser's own speech recognition, and on most browsers that is a cloud service run by the browser's maker — Chrome sends audio to Google, Safari and every browser on an iPhone or iPad send it to Apple. If that matters to your table, leave table-talk recording off.

Where the text goes

Transcribed text is sent to whichever language model the person running this server configured — Google Gemini by default, or Anthropic's Claude, or a model on their own network. Voices are generated on this server; no voice samples are taken from anyone.

What is stored

Per table: a running memory, a timeline of events, corrections (with the name of whoever made them), a cast list and places, the session logs of questions and answers, the campaign notes and the familiar's persona, and a search index over all of it. Per person: account holders have an email address and a hashed password; players who joined by invitation have only the name they typed. Token and character counts are kept per table per month — numbers, not text. There are no analytics or third-party scripts, and the only cookie is the one that keeps you signed in.

Who can see it

Everyone at your table can read and correct the record. The campaign notes are visible only to the DM. The person running this server can read everything on it — it is their machine.

Getting it back, and getting rid of it

A table's owner or DM can download everything the table has recorded as a single archive, and can delete the table outright — immediately and irreversibly. Leaving somebody else's table removes your membership; the record of what happened stays with the table. To delete your account (and the tables you own), ask whoever runs this server.

Telling your table

Consent is not something a DM can give on behalf of five other people. The invitation page says what recording means before anyone accepts, and the recording switch is visible on every device while it is on. If somebody does not want to be recorded, leave the switch off — everything else works exactly the same without it.
